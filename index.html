Research vision

Turning genomic evidence into research priorities

OryzaGen is an independent computational research project connecting rice gene-expression responses, published salinity-associated genomic regions and machine learning evidence.

The challenge

Rice salinity research produces expression measurements, genetic variants and trait observations in separate datasets. Bringing these sources together can help researchers decide which genes deserve further study.

The pitch deck places this work within a broader vision of climate-resilient agriculture. The current analysis focuses on salt stress in rice; it does not directly measure climate-change effects.

The research aim

Develop a reproducible framework that organizes these complementary signals into a transparent candidate-gene priority list. This can support the selection of targets for laboratory studies and future breeding research.

Computational ranking is an early discovery step. Gene function, crop performance and breeding value require experimental and field evaluation.

Research workflow

From input data to interpretable outputs

The analysis runs in the research notebook. This public dashboard presents selected results, figures and downloadable tables.

  1. Prepare the data

    Clean the supplied GEO expression data, examine the PLINK genotype panel and align public salinity phenotypes with accession metadata. Quality control and exploratory plots provide the context for interpretation.

  2. Integrate the evidence

    Rank saline-versus-control expression changes and cross-reference gene coordinates with published salinity GWAS loci using a ±100 kb window. Compare machine learning models under population-blocked validation to assess prediction across population groups.

  3. Review the candidates

    Combine the expression ranking, nearby published GWAS support and available SNP-neighborhood evidence in a candidate priority table. Inspect genomic coordinates, model metrics and figures before selecting genes for biological follow-up.

01 · Evidence

Three complementary signals

The strongest result is convergence: genes that respond to salinity and lie near independently published salinity GWAS loci receive higher priority.

Transcriptomic response

Expression changes provide the biological response layer. Candidates are ranked by effect size and expression evidence.

03

Method note

Designed for transparent prioritization

OryzaGen integrates salinity-responsive expression features, published salinity-associated GWAS coordinates using a ±100 kb genomic window, and population-blocked machine learning to prioritize candidate genes for rice salinity tolerance. The modelling layer compares baseline, regularized logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost approaches.

The prediction target is high versus low germination index under 60 mM NaCl among the selected extreme-class accessions. Expression effect sizes and GWAS proximity support prioritization; they do not establish statistically significant differential expression or causal gene function on their own.

Potential applications

Supporting the next research decision

The pitch deck describes potential uses across plant research, crop improvement and agricultural biotechnology.

Plant genomics research

Explore salt-response candidates, inspect the evidence behind each ranking and select genes for functional experiments.

Breeding and biotechnology

Provide an initial shortlist for marker-assisted selection or gene-editing research, subject to independent validation.

Agricultural resilience

Contribute an interpretable research resource for studying salinity tolerance, with wider food-security applications as a long-term aim.

Development roadmap

A research foundation with room to grow

The completed analysis provides a starting point for the broader computational framework described in the pitch deck.

  1. Current prototype

    Integrated candidate prioritization

    Notebook-based expression analysis, published GWAS integration, population-blocked model comparison and a public results dashboard.

  2. Planned development

    Interactive analysis and stronger validation

    Add researcher dataset uploads and backend processing, then assess reproducibility with independent data and biological validation. These analysis services are not available on this static dashboard.

  3. Future research

    Deep learning and multi-crop expansion

    Investigate sequence-based Transformer or graph neural network models if suitable data become available. Explore extensions to wheat and maize after validating the rice workflow.
